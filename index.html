What we stand for

Many treatments that could help people with rare diseases are already known, scattered across case reports and journals no single doctor has time to read. We believe AI can find them faster and more cheaply than the old way, without animal testing. We do not fund or promote animal testing.

This site shares published evidence. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or replace a qualified practitioner.

Find a doctor

Find someone who knows your condition and will listen.

With a rare disease, the right practitioner matters more than the nearest one. Below are trusted places to start, for conventional and holistic care. We don't run these services or get paid by them.

Rare-disease experts

  • NORD Rare Disease Centers of Excellence

    United States

    A network of academic medical centers that care for people with any rare disease, including complex and unusual cases.

  • GARD: Genetic and Rare Diseases Information Center

    United States · NIH · free

    Free information specialists who can help you find specialists for your disease, in English or Spanish. They don't give medical advice.

  • Find a Genetic Counselor (NSGC)

    United States and Canada · in person or telehealth

    Genetic counselors explain test results and inheritance, and often know which specialists see your condition.

  • Undiagnosed Diseases Network

    United States · research study

    For people still without a diagnosis after a thorough workup. You apply online, and you'll need a signed letter from a healthcare provider.

  • European Reference Networks

    EU, Norway, Ukraine · through your doctor

    Networks of specialist hospitals whose expert panels review difficult cases. Your treating doctor makes the referral, so bring them a clear summary.

  • Orphanet expert centres

    Europe and beyond

    Search your disease, then look for its expert centres. You can filter by country and by adult or children's care.

Holistic and integrative practitioners

Some people find relief from approaches their conventional doctors never mention. These directories list practitioners who hold a recognized certification.

  • Institute for Functional Medicine: Find a Practitioner

    Worldwide

    Medical doctors, osteopaths, chiropractors, nutritionists and others trained in functional medicine, which looks for root causes across body systems.

  • Board-certified acupuncture and herbal medicine (NCBAHM)

    United States

    Nationally board-certified acupuncturists and Chinese herbalists. Listing is voluntary, so also check your state's licensing board.

  • Chiropractors and other licensed practitioners

    Your state or country

    Search "[your state] board of chiropractic" (or acupuncture, naturopathy, massage) to confirm a license and see any complaints.

Tell every practitioner your diagnosis.

Some rare diseases change what's safe. For example, neck manipulation carries extra risk for people with connective-tissue disorders, and some herbs interact with prescription drugs. A good practitioner will want to know.

Questions that show whether a practitioner will help

  1. Have you cared for anyone with my condition, or one like it?
  2. If I bring published case reports, will you read them and tell me what you think?
  3. Will you work with my other doctors and practitioners, conventional and holistic?
  4. If you can't help, can you refer me to someone who can?
  5. Do you offer telehealth, so distance isn't a barrier?

What to bring

  • Your diagnosis and any genetic test results.
  • A list of everything you take: prescriptions, supplements, herbs.
  • What you've tried, and what helped or didn't.
  • Your one-page evidence summary Coming
